# RealSignal 5.6.9 — IA depth and cleanup report

## Where we are

The IA inventory contains **170 canonical stations and 13 legacy aliases**. No stations are removed, hidden or renumbered by this update. OK, Vimeo and Roku are unchanged.

This is a targeted depth release and an all-IA diagnostic baseline, **not a claim that all IA channels are fully repaired or first-frame certified**. A catalog can be large while only a small subset has current transport evidence. The ready shelf is a small playback buffer, not the catalog limit.

## Changes in this release

- Classic Cartoons: **605 → 3917** metadata-qualified, deduplicated files; **30 → 83** declared show families; **91** reviewed parent collections. Includes **2333 complete original cartoons shorter than 15 minutes**. Reviewed originals have a two-minute floor; ordinary TV and feature-film floors are unchanged.
- Landscape, runtime and source checks remain. Short-form admission is confined to reviewed original-cartoon collections; clips, trailers, DVD introductions, podcasts, fan/parody productions and portrait files remain excluded.
- Alternate commercial-edition and derivative filenames no longer manufacture extra fresh episodes. Actual delivery filenames remain intact.
- Session history survives upgrading a bare collection ID to its exact episode-file identity. This fixes a reproduced early-repeat defect in the guarded IA path.
- Comedy Archive combines sketch and stand-up without retaining their mutually exclusive format bans. Trailer, review, podcast and other contamination exclusions are preserved. Other merged-channel denial policies retain their union.
- Deep repair catalogs remain server-side. Tune responses expose a moving window of at most 512 candidates plus the small ready shelf, while the deeper retained catalog is not truncated by that wire limit.
- Static reviewed metadata and normalized policy terms are reused rather than repeatedly rebuilt. Policy edits invalidate normalized-term reuse. This shared editorial optimization applies wherever IA ranking uses these helpers.

## Evidence and limits

- The complete pre-change three-snapshot queue survey had a ready shelf on **170/170 canonical stations**. That measures queue availability, not decoded playback.
- The later deployed-mode audit below exercised up to 12 queue selections and skip reports per canonical station, at concurrency two. **143/170 completed all 12 rounds; 20 lanes repeated a selected identity; 41 returned at least one shelf containing a missing media URL.** Missing URLs can trigger client-side hydration; they are not proof of an observed dead screen. They show why queue readiness must not be confused with verified immediate playback.
- No HTTP failures or skip-report endpoint failures occurred in that audit; the recorded admission failures were missing media URLs. Not all 170 stations use the guarded file-level player: only 10, 12, 22 and 150 are currently promoted. Other stations retain their existing production path.
- The local cartoon canary completed **96 acknowledged skips with no selected repeats**, spanning **50 title families**, with three transport-qualified ready items throughout. This is not 96 watched/decoded episodes.
- A bounded MP4-header survey passed for 3,932 researched file URLs before editorial deduplication and extra removal. Header validation is not full decode certification.
- Browser checks confirmed real moving landscape video, Next switching to a different cartoon, and Guide current/next data. Physical mobile and Cast were not certified in this run.
- Post-deployment checks: 12 priority stations completed 144 selections and skip reports, with 144 unique selections, zero selected repeats and zero missing-media results. Coverage: 10, 11, 12, 22, 150, 202 and 704–709. The public desktop showed 5.6.9 and decoded video both before and after Next. API, relay, Pages and CI release workflows all passed.
- Modern Game Shows had local cold-canary requests exceeding the 25-second test deadline even though later server traces showed a three-item response. This remains an open latency finding, not a successful cold-start certification.
- The subsequent hosted Modern Game Shows test passed all 12 rotations; first queue response was 373 ms, median 201 ms. This narrows the earlier timeout finding to the local cold canary; it does not establish first-visible-frame speed on every device.
- Local synchronous metadata selection: cold 543.93 ms for the larger cartoon bank; warm 0.06 ms maximum in five samples. Wire catalog JSON 730039 → 612180 bytes. These are local selector measurements, not Cloudflare CPU billing or end-user first-frame timings.

## Next goals, in order

1. Finish the Modern Game Shows cold-path investigation without discarding its deeper catalog or weakening admission.
2. Convert metadata-only ready slots into background-verified files, preserving a playable fallback. Promote additional IA families only after their real starts, skips and recovery pass.
3. Prioritize repeat-heavy rows below. Expand complete-series siblings and new show families, not alternate encodings or repeated first episodes.
4. Apply the same measured depth work to holiday animation/TV/films, reruns, movies, outdoor and music archives. Keep each holiday lane and the protected special/Black-media channels separate.
5. Measure family balance and multiple viewing rotations as well as queue depth. Require visible frames before claiming playback certification. No channel-hiding or unrelated feature work.

The goal is a continuously growing, refreshable catalog—not a promise of infinite inventory or immunity from Archive outages.
