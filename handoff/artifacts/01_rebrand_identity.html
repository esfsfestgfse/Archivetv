The Name

"Afterglow" is what the tube does when you cut the power — the residual light that hangs on the phosphor for a beat before the screen goes dark. It's the ghost of the last channel. It's why late-night TV feels haunted.

— The name explains itself

One word

Not "The [X] Channel" or "[Something] TV". A single evocative noun that means something before it's an app.

On brand

The existing channel roster already leans nocturnal — Nite-Owl 3, The Late Late Show, The Up-All-Night, Fallout Radio. Afterglow is what you watch there.

Instant logo

The wordmark writes itself: a phosphor dot with decaying halo rings. Works at any size. Works animated. Works as a favicon.

Search-clean

"Afterglow" is a common word but not a common app name. Distinctive enough to own, familiar enough to remember. Not another blue-gradient AI startup.

Palette · Four Colors

The phosphor speaks amber.

A committed dark-first palette. Amber (P3 phosphor) as the single accent — no competing hues. Warm-shifted neutrals so the app feels like a real object, not a website. Semantic greens/reds/blues stay on the tube, not on the chrome.

Type · Three Voices

Condensed sans, softened by a serif, marked by a monospace.

The condensed sans is the app's operational voice — chrome, buttons, the SA readout, the guide labels. Georgia carries the reading — user guide prose, description copy, splash text. Courier appears only where numbers live: timestamps, hex codes, channel numbers on the technical side.
